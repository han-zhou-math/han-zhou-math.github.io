Research statement

I study how fluids interact with deformable membranes, filaments, and cells. My work combines mathematical modeling, numerical simulation, and PDE analysis, with an emphasis on moving interfaces with open edges and junctions.

Read the full statement (PDF)

Research directions

Membrane biophysics in parasitology

I model how a ruptured host-cell membrane changes shape. I use open-membrane models in Stokes flow to study how membrane elasticity, line tension, and fluid dissipation affect this motion.

Multiphysics modeling and computation of cell motility

My collaborators and I use Cartesian-grid methods on moving domains to study how osmotic flow and actin dynamics interact during cell migration.

Mathematical theory of fluid–structure interaction

I study well-posedness, regularity near free edges, and long-time dynamics for deformable interfaces, including inextensible filaments in Stokes flow.
